Fantasy Premier League: what drives points?

An exploratory analysis of captaincy, player value, bonus points, set-piece responsibility and how team ELO ratings compare with FPL points.

Dataset: supplied FPL master CSV, 2025-26 season · 38 gameweeks · 841 players · 29,978 player-gameweek records. Findings are descriptive, not causal. Data source: FPL Core Insights on GitHub

Did it pay off? The most-captained players scored 229 points across the season before captaincy doubling. That's 458 captain points if the popular pick was captained every week. The returns were useful, but not reliable every round.

Who scored more over the season?

Bruno Fernandes was the highest-scoring player other than Haaland. He finished on 235 points, just four behind Haaland's 239. Haaland edged him over the full season, but the gap was small.

Despite finishing top overall, Haaland wasn't the highest-scoring player in any individual gameweek. Big single-week returns came from other players, which is why choosing a captain based on the fixture and likely performance matters.

The takeaway: Haaland was the strongest overall scorer, but not the weekly top scorer. Bruno Fernandes came close across the season, and a good captaincy decision needs to focus on who is most likely to deliver in the next gameweek.
02 · Player value

Are defenders, midfielders or forwards better value?

Value is total season points divided by the player's latest listed price (£m). The comparison includes all defenders, midfielders and forwards, including players with limited minutes; goalkeepers are excluded.

Average vs median points per £1m

Best all-round value: defenders. They return 9.48 average points per £1m, ahead of midfielders (7.22) and forwards (6.94). They also have the highest median return, so their lead is not just down to a few standout players.

Average and typical returns tell different stories. Midfielders beat forwards on average value (7.22 vs 6.94), but forwards have the higher median (3.75 vs 2.86). This suggests midfielder returns are more uneven, with high-value midfielders likely influencing the average.
There is value in defenders. 52.6% of defenders returned at least 5 points per £1m, compared with 46.2% of midfielders and 44.2% of forwards. Defenders are the strongest position for finding players who can deliver 5+ points per £1m.
03 · Performance drivers

Which stats are most associated with total points?

Pearson correlations across each player's latest season snapshot (841 players). Goals, assists, bonus, BPS, total points as a candidate metric, direct scoring events and value measures are excluded. ICT indicators and expected metrics are included. Correlation shows association, not cause.

Strongest correlations with total points

What this means for FPL decisions

Start with reliable minutes. Minutes played and starts are among the strongest signals in this dataset. Before choosing between similar players, check who is most likely to start and stay on the pitch.
Use ICT metrics as a quick attacking-performance screen. Influence and ICT Index rank highly, while Threat and Creativity help describe different parts of a player's involvement. Use them to shortlist players, then check fixtures and expected minutes.
Bring expected metrics into the comparison. xG, xA and xGI can help identify players who are getting into promising positions or creating chances, even when recent returns have been modest.
Don't overlook defensive work. Defensive contributions, recoveries, tackles and clearances/blocks/interceptions are positively associated with season points. They can help separate players whose attacking numbers look similar.
Interpret the rankings carefully. Season totals naturally rise with playing time, and several metrics overlap. These correlations are useful for screening players, not proof that a metric independently predicts future points.

Methodology & limitations

How to read this analysis

Data preparationFour supplied CSVs joined into the master file. Season-level comparisons use the latest available player snapshot; gameweek captaincy uses event points.
LimitationsSome figures are season-to-date snapshots, player/team attribution can change after transfers, and set-piece comparisons show patterns rather than proving cause and effect. Aggregate FPL points are a useful comparison, but they are not the same as league points or match results.

Data source: the FPL CSV files supplied for this project. ClubELO is represented by the ELO field in the supplied teams table; ratings have not been independently checked against the ClubELO website.

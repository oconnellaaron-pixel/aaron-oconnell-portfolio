Why I tested multiple signals together

One feature on its own can be misleading and may create unnecessary friction for genuine customers. I wanted to test whether combining signals could separate higher-risk applications more effectively.

How I would use the results

I tested three models on the synthetic dataset. The workflow below shows how I would assess a model in practice, balancing fraud detection with the impact on genuine customers.

Step 1 · Inputs

Combine signals

Device patterns, credit-limit request, phone checks, email connections, application details and other available features.

Step 2 · Model

Estimate risk together

Learn how signals work in combination, rather than treating any one flag as automatic proof of fraud.

Step 3 · Decision

Apply proportionate action

Route applications to approve, step-up verification or manual review based on risk and the cost of getting it wrong.

Step 4 · Measure

Track both outcomes

Measure fraud caught alongside false positives, genuine-customer drop-off, review volumes and approval rates.

What success looks like

More fraud detected while keeping genuine customers moving through onboarding with as little unnecessary friction as possible.

What to validate before launch

Test on unseen data, compare results with the current process, check performance across customer groups, and monitor results after release.

The models I tested

I trained and compared CatBoost, LightGBM and Logistic Regression, using an earlier period for training and a later period to test how well each model performed on data it had not seen. CatBoost gave the strongest overall results in this test.

What these results mean: I ran these tests on a synthetic dataset, so the results do not show how the models would perform in a live bank. The threshold affects how many applications are flagged, how much fraud is caught and how many genuine customers could be delayed. I would choose a model based on its results alongside the cost and operational impact, not on one score alone.

What I would do next with real data

My next step would be to find out whether the approach adds value in a real setting, and whether the expected savings justify the cost and operational changes.

Step 1 · Improve the data

Look for additional features

I would work with Product, Fraud and Data teams to identify useful signals we do not currently have, check whether the data is reliable and available, and test whether it improves detection.

Step 2 · Build the business case

Estimate costs and savings

I would estimate the fraud losses the model could prevent and compare that with false-positive costs, verification spend, manual-review effort and any drop in genuine-customer conversion.

Step 3 · Check the impact

Understand operational changes

I would assess review volumes, decision times, verification steps, system integration and team capacity. I would also agree what should happen when an application falls into each risk level.

Step 4 · Prove it works

Run a controlled pilot

I would compare the model with the current process on real, unseen data. If the results justify it, I would recommend a limited pilot with clear success measures and ongoing monitoring before any wider rollout.

The key question

Would the fraud losses prevented outweigh the cost of running the model and any extra friction for genuine customers?

What I would need to recommend rollout

A clear estimate of net savings, customer impact and operational capacity, with agreed criteria for deciding whether to proceed.
